# Equity Linked Note (ELN)

## How it works
An ELN pays a fixed coupon and links the repayment of principal to an underlying index or share. If the underlying never falls below the barrier, the investor gets the principal back at maturity. If the barrier is breached and the final level ends below the strike, the investor receives principal multiplied by final level divided by initial level, so part of the invested money is lost.

## Barrier explained
The barrier (knock-in level) is a percentage of the initial level, for example 70%. A 70% barrier means the note is at risk once the underlying has fallen 30% from its starting level. A lower barrier gives more cushion; a higher barrier usually pays a higher coupon but is more likely to be breached.

## Main risks
Capital is not protected. The coupon is fixed and limited, while the downside can be large after a barrier breach. Gains from a rising market are capped at the coupon. The note may be hard to sell before maturity, and the issuer's credit risk also applies.

## Plain language
If the market falls a lot, you can lose part of your money. If it does not fall that far, you get your money back plus the fixed coupon.
